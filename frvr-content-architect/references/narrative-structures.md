# Narrative structures that have stood the test of time

Each one is mapped to Root to Fruit and to the existing FRVR spine (**Hook → Turn → Tool → Anchor → CTA**) and carousel concepts (C1–C16 in `frvr-design-studio/references/carousel-concepts.md`). Choose the structure from how the follower stands toward the problem.

| He… | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| doesn't know he has the problem | **Nathan's Parable** | He recognises the story before he sees himself in it |
| knows the symptom, not the cause | **The Descent** (diagnosis) | Walk the why-ladder with him |
| knows the cause and feels stuck | **Lament → Trust** | Honest complaint, then the turn |
| believes the world's answer | **Upside Down** (world says / kingdom says) | The maxxing flip |
| is ready to act | **Ladder** (Floor → Standard → Stretch) | Smallest step first |
| needs to see it's possible | **Testimony** (before → turn → after) | The pattern lived by someone like him |

## 1. Nathan's Parable (2 Samuel 12:1–7)
Story → his verdict → "you are the man" → the turn → the way back.
Slides: 1 a scene he recognises (no moral yet) · 2–3 the story builds · 4 the question: "what would you say to him?" · 5 the reveal: "most of us are him" (gentle, first person plural) · 6 the root named · 7 the verse · 8 the frame · 9 the first step · 10 CTA.
Best for: problems he can't see (busyness as avoidance, comparison, quiet self-reliance). Guardrail: "we", not "you"; reveal, never shame.

## 2. The Descent (diagnosis)
Fruit → Pattern → Pain → Lie → Root → Longing → Truth → Frame → Tool → CTA. The canonical Root to Fruit carousel. It maps to C3. Worked example in `root-to-fruit.md` §4.

## 3. Lament → Trust (Psalm 13)
Complaint → petition → trust → praise. It maps to C9. Best for pain that's real and current. Testimony is Aziel's to tell; always include the signpost.

## 4. Upside Down
The world says (the maxxing answer, stated fairly) → why it can't deliver (the root it misses) → the kingdom says → the protocol from finished identity → anchor. It maps to `frvr-maxxing-studio`'s spine (Hook → Flip → Turn → Tool → Trend vs Truth → Anchor → CTA).

## 5. The Ladder
Where you are → why the big plan fails → Floor → Standard → Stretch → the if-then plan → grace clause → CTA. Best for Discipleship posts and Blueprints. It must have a Descent behind it, either in this post or earlier in the series (linked).

## 6. Testimony (before → turn → after)
Before (honest, specific) → the moment (what he saw) → the truth that met him → what he did (protocol) → what changed (real numbers, misses included) → Christ gets the credit. It maps to C5 and C16, for FRVR AZ.

## 7. Creation → Fall → Redemption → Restoration (the master arc)
Design (how you were made: the science of thriving, Genesis 1–2) → Distortion (what broke) → Deliverance (Christ) → Discipleship (formation). Use it as a single carousel for big topics (sleep, work, sex, rest), and as the shape of every series and season.

## 8. The Question (Jesus' method)
Open with a question, not a claim ("Do you want to get well?", John 5:6; "Who do you say I am?", Mark 8:29). Each slide asks a deeper question. The truth answers the last one. Best for reels and talking heads; it invites comments.

## Rules that hold across all of them
- **One argument, said in five sentences.** If you can't summarise the post as five linked sentences, the logic isn't ready.
- **Each slide earns the next.** Every slide ends with an open loop: a question, a "but", a tension.
- **One idea per slide, and every slide does one job** from one act.
- **The turn sits about two-thirds of the way through.** Too early and the Descent is shallow. Too late and the Ascent is rushed.
- **The Anchor is Christ, not the protocol.** The last word before the CTA is about him.
