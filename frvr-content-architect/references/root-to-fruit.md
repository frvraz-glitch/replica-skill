# Root to Fruit: the method in depth

## 1. Why this shape

- **It is how Jesus reads people.** He moves from fruit to root ("out of the overflow of the heart the mouth speaks", Luke 6:45) and from outside to inside (Mark 7:14–23). He asks questions before he gives answers (Mark 8:27–29; John 5:6, "Do you want to get well?"). Fetch the BSB wording before setting any of these.
- **It is how the prophets confront.** Nathan tells David a story, David judges it, then he hears "You are the man" (2 Samuel 12:1–7). The listener recognises the problem before he realises it is his. That is how we reach a problem he doesn't know he has.
- **It is how good diagnosis works.** Root-cause analysis (the "5 Whys" from Toyota's production system) separates symptoms from causes. Fix a symptom and it comes back; fix the cause and the fruit changes.
- **It is how people change.** Behaviour-change research consistently finds that knowing *what* to do isn't enough. Durable change links a behaviour to identity, meaning and environment. Verify the specific claims in `frvr-evidence-library` before teaching them. Concepts worth checking: identity-based motivation (Oyserman), self-determination theory (Deci and Ryan), implementation intentions (Gollwitzer and Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis), habit formation timing (Lally et al., 2010).
- **The gospel order guards it.** Identity is received first (Ephesians 2:8–10: saved by grace, *for* good works). So the Ascent always begins with Truth and ends with works done *from* acceptance, never *for* it.

## 2. The why-ladder

Start with the sentence he would actually say. Ask "why?" or "what's underneath that?" until you reach a **lie** (a belief) and a **root** (what he trusts, loves or fears most). That usually takes four to six rungs. Stop when the next "why" lands on a desire God made good. That is the **longing**.

Run each rung through the four faces. A surface problem in one face often has its root in another.

| Rung | Prompt | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Fruit | "What does he see? What would he type into search?" | Use his words, not ours |
| Pattern | "When does it happen? What comes just before? What does it give him?" | Find the cue and the payoff |
| Pain | "What does he feel right before, and right after?" | Name the emotion precisely: not "bad" but "restless", "ashamed", "invisible" |
| Lie | "What must he believe for this to make sense?" | Write it in first person: "I am…", "God is…", "If I…, then…" |
| Root | "What is he trusting to save him, or fearing will undo him?" | Usually identity, worth, control, belonging or safety |
| Longing | "What good thing is he really reaching for?" | It must be good in itself. The longing is never the sin |

**Branches.** A fruit can have more than one root. Keep up to three branches and pick the one that is most common and most hidden for this post. The others become later posts in the series.

**Science at each rung.** Give the mechanism as a named concept and send every specific claim to the evidence library: for example, the stress response and sleep loss (Pain), variable reward and habit loops (Pattern), core beliefs in cognitive models (Lie). Never let a mechanism replace the root. Biology describes *how* it happens. Scripture names *what* it is and *who* restores it.

## 3. The Ascent ladder

| Stage | Write it as | Must |
|---|---|---|
| Truth | "The truth is…": one sentence that answers the lie directly, then the verse, whole, in context | Come from Scripture, be about God before it is about him, and be received rather than achieved |
| Frame | "So instead of seeing X as…, see it as…" | Flip the world's frame (the upside-down kingdom) and fit on one line |
| Framework | A named model: the Temple, Floor/Standard/Stretch, Flee and Pursue, the lament arc, a 2×2 | Be simple enough to draw on a whiteboard in 10 seconds |
| Formation | The practice and the protocol | Pass the SMART builder (`protocol-builder.md`) and include an if-then plan |
| Fruit | "In 7/30/49 days, you'll know it's working when…" | Give a measure, a review date and the way back in grace (1 John 1:9; Lamentations 3:22–23). "Never miss twice" is a practical rhythm under grace, never a condition of it |

## 4. Worked example: "I can't stay consistent"

**Descent**
- **Fruit:** "I start strong every Monday and quit by Thursday." (Search terms: "how to stay consistent", "discipline", "I keep restarting".)
- **Pattern:** Big plan → one missed day → "I've ruined it" → quit → binge comfort (phone, food, porn) → shame → new Monday plan.
- **Pain:** Shame, then the low hum of feeling like a fraud. Restlessness.
- **Lie:** "If I miss, I've failed. I am only as good as my streak." Underneath that: "God is waiting for me to get it together."
- **Root:** Identity built on performance. The plan is a way of earning worth, so any miss is a verdict on him, not just a missed day.
- **Longing:** To be a man who is steady and trusted, and to be accepted before he performs.

**Ascent**
- **Truth:** Your standing with God is finished, not on probation (John 19:30; Romans 8:1, fetch the BSB). You don't train *for* acceptance; you train *from* it.
- **Frame:** A missed day is not a verdict. It's a fork in the road: shame takes you back to the start, grace takes you back to the floor.
- **Framework:** Floor / Standard / Stretch. Unbroken: never miss twice.
- **Formation:** Choose one habit. Floor = 2 minutes (one push-up set, one verse read). Standard = the real target. Stretch = a good day. If-then: "If I miss a day, then the next morning I do the floor before my phone."
- **Fruit:** For 49 days (Life Reset 7x7), track "floor hit" Y/N. Review every Sunday. Success means no two misses in a row, not a perfect streak.
